A Marcus field report

The AI handed you a source. We clicked it.

We gave four AI search engines a sentence from each of 200 real news articles and asked the same question every time: which article is this from? That produced 800 citations: headline, publisher, date, link. We knew the true source of every sentence, so we graded each answer and clicked every link to see whether it even exists.

What the engines returned, unchecked · 800 answers, four engines
69.5%of the answers came back wrong
And 51% of every link they cited was fabricated, dead, or pointed to the wrong source. A made-up link looks exactly like a real one. You cannot tell which until you click it, and nobody clicks.

So with any AI citation you get two choices: trust it blind, or re-check every one by hand. We did the second: we clicked all 800. Here is what came back, three exhibits straight from the run’s audit records.

The test re-runs the Tow Center’s 2025 study, “AI Search Has a Citation Problem.” And citations are only the version of this that is easy to measure: the same failure hides in any AI answer you act on without a way to check it, such as numbers in a report, legal references, medical claims, or code.

The fix isn’t a smarter model

So we made them show their work.

An answer that might be confidently wrong is an answer you cannot use: someone has to re-check it by hand, which cancels the point of asking an AI at all. So we changed the rules instead of the model. Same engines, same questions, now passing through Marcus: open-source software that works as a checkpoint between the AI and you, enforcing three rules nobody can skip.

1Evidence, or it doesn’t count. An answer is not accepted until every receipt is present: headline, publisher, date, link.
2Every claim is checked. Never by its author. All claims must be verified, and the check runs as a deterministic script from a different vendor. The author is never even shown the checking task, because an author who knows how it will be graded can shape its answer to pass the check instead of to be true. Hidden from the author, the only winning move is honesty.
3People hold the final signature. People do not sign at every step: the scripts do the checking, at a scale no reviewer could (800 link fetches in this study). A person signs at the end, on a sampled set of already-verified cases, and that signature can only belong to a human. Software can never wave its own work through, and someone is always answerable for what shipped.

The fix used no retraining and no prompt begging for honesty. The environment simply stopped letting unverified claims ship, and when a link cannot be verified, it declines with the receipt instead of guessing. Here is what survives.

What you actually gain

Before, wrong links were invisible.

Until now you had two options with an AI’s citation: accept it blindly, or re-check every one yourself. And when one was wrong, nothing anywhere recorded that it was wrong, or why.

thenBlind trust, or check everything by hand. A citation arrived as bare text. Wrong ones looked identical to right ones, and when one burned you, no record existed of what was claimed or why it failed.
nowEvery answer leaves a record, especially the wrong ones. Exhibits A through C are not reconstructions; they are the captured evidence itself. The claim, the fetch, the 404, the human signature: the reason an answer was or was not trusted is on file, case by case.

That is the trade. You accept fewer answers, and in exchange every answer you do accept has survived its checks, and every answer you refuse comes with the documented reason why. Trust stops being a feeling and becomes a lookup.

Say that plainly: once a verification step exists and the caught claims are declined with receipts, only about 1 in 8 answers that actually reached you carried any error, and only about 1 in 29 was flatly wrong. Without it, about 7 in 10 were.

The engines are exactly as wrong as before; the caught claims arrive as labeled declines instead of answers. The record carries a cost: a small multiple on tokens alone, fractions of a cent per answer, and grounded engines bill far more for search than token logging captures. The effect held across every vendor and price tier tested, frontier models included.

Marcus is open source. The preregistration, the gates, the failed first pilot and the passing re-run are all in the repository history, and every case shown above exports its full audit bundle: who claimed, who checked, the evidence, who signed, what it cost. And nothing in it is citation-specific. The checkpoint works for any claim a script can check: a number against its source, code against its tests, a legal citation against the record. News citations were simply the demo with a published baseline to replicate.
