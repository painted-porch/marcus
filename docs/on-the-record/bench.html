Verifier Bench

In a live replication of Columbia’s Tow Center study, AI search engines handed us wrong answers 73.8% of the time, with fabricated citations. This bench shows how a verifier they can’t skip catches them. Three steps, two minutes.

1 · Catch one lie 2 · Run the verifier 3 · Now break it

We gave an AI search engine a sentence from a real news article and asked: where is this from? Here is its answer.

the question
“Some good news is that this storm should make a lasting impact…” — which article is this from? (fragment only; the dataset’s excerpts stay protected)
confident answer 404 NOT FOUND

“New England storm brings flooding rain”

The Boston Globe · December 11, 2024
bostonglobe.com/2024/12/11/metro/storm-flooding-rain-drought/

The page does not exist. The engine didn’t say “I’m not sure”; it invented a plausible link. In our test, 54.6% of cited URLs failed this one fetch. Nobody clicks every link. That’s the problem.

You catch them with a verifier: five dumb, deterministic checks, run on every answer by someone who is never the answer’s author. No AI judging AI. Just receipts.
